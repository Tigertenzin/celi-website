# Privacy Policy

**Waypoint Journal**
_Last updated: 2 October 2026_

Waypoint Journal is an app for iPhone, iPad, Apple Watch and Mac that saves the places you visit, so
you can add them to your journal later. This policy explains what happens to your information when you
use it.

## The short version

- **We don't collect your personal data.** Waypoint Journal has no accounts, no sign-up, no analytics,
  no advertising, and no tracking. It contains no third-party SDKs.
- **Your places are yours.** They're stored on your devices and, if you use iCloud, in your own private
  iCloud storage. We can't see them.
- **Apple helps find place names.** To suggest what's nearby, the app asks Apple Maps about a location.
  That request goes to Apple, never to us.

## What Waypoint Journal stores, and where

Waypoint Journal stores the things you save and the choices you make in the app:

- your waypoints: where and when each was saved, how accurate the position was, what saved it (for
  example the app, a widget, or your Apple Watch), and the place name, category, address and notes you
  give it
- automatic visits your iPhone noticed, if you turn them on
- the nearby place suggestions for each waypoint
- your saved places
- your settings, such as your export format and template, when your day ends, and your app icon

This information is stored **on your device**. On iPhone and iPad it's also kept in a storage area shared
with the Waypoint Journal widgets and controls on the same device, so they can save places and show your
day without opening the app.

### iCloud sync

If you're signed in to iCloud, Waypoint Journal syncs your waypoints and saved places between your
devices using Apple's CloudKit, in **your private iCloud database**, and syncs your settings using iCloud
key-value storage. This data belongs to your iCloud account. We, the developer, have no access to it: we
can't read it, copy it, or share it. Apple protects and processes iCloud data under
[Apple's Privacy Policy](https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/).

To stop syncing, turn off iCloud for Waypoint Journal in **Settings › [your name] › iCloud** (on a Mac,
**System Settings › [your name] › iCloud**).

## Permissions Waypoint Journal may ask for

- **Location (While Using the App):** to save where you are when you press capture. Your position is
  read once for each capture. It isn't tracked continuously.
- **Location (Always), optional:** only if you turn on automatic visits. Your iPhone then tells Waypoint
  Journal about places where you stopped for a while. These visits stay on your device and in your
  iCloud, hidden from your journal until you review them. Turn automatic visits off at any time in the
  app, or change location access in **Settings › Privacy & Security › Location Services**.
- **Journaling Suggestions (iPhone):** if you choose to import places from Apple's Journaling
  Suggestions, iOS shows its own picker. Waypoint Journal only receives the places you pick, and never
  sees the rest of the suggestions.
- **Notifications (Mac):** only if you add days to Obsidian by opening the note to paste. A notification
  then tells you the day is ready to paste. It's created on your Mac and never leaves it.
- **A folder on your Mac:** only if you add days to Obsidian. You choose your Obsidian vault's folder, and
  Waypoint Journal can only reach that folder. It reads Obsidian's daily note settings there, and, if you
  choose to have it write into your notes, edits the day's daily note. It doesn't read or change
  anything else in your vault.

iCloud sync may use Apple's silent push notifications to let your devices know there's something new to
sync. These are never shown to you and carry no personal information.

## What's shared with Apple to find places

To suggest the names of nearby places, find a street address, search for a place, and show maps,
Waypoint Journal uses Apple's MapKit. This sends the location or search text involved to Apple Maps.
Apple handles these requests under [Apple's Privacy Policy](https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/).
We never receive them.

## Sharing and exporting

When you export a day, Waypoint Journal creates the text or file on your device. You choose where it
goes: you can copy it, pass it to the share sheet, return it to a Shortcuts automation, or, on the Mac,
add it to your Obsidian daily note. From then on, the app or service you send it to handles it under its
own privacy policy.

Exports contain the places and positions you saved, so think about where you share them.

## Information Apple may share with us

If you've chosen to share analytics with app developers in your device's settings (on iPhone,
**Settings › Privacy & Security › Analytics & Improvements**), Apple may give us crash reports and
aggregated usage statistics. Apple doesn't identify you in them, and we use them only to find and fix
problems in Waypoint Journal. You can turn this sharing off in the same place at any time.

TestFlight testers may also send us feedback and screenshots through TestFlight. We only receive what you
choose to send, and use it only to improve the app.

## Children

Waypoint Journal isn't directed at children under 13, and we don't knowingly collect personal information
from anyone, including children. Because the app collects no personal data, there's none to collect from
children either.

## Keeping and deleting your data

- **Delete a waypoint, a day or a saved place:** you can delete any of them in the app. If you use
  iCloud, they're deleted from your other devices too.
- **Delete everything on a device:** deleting Waypoint Journal removes its data from that device.
- **Delete your iCloud data:** go to **Settings › [your name] › iCloud › Manage Account Storage**,
  choose Waypoint Journal, and delete its data.

Since we never receive your data, we have nothing to keep or delete on our side.

## Your privacy rights

Depending on where you live, you may have rights over your personal data, such as the right to access,
correct, or delete it. For example, these rights may come from the GDPR in the EU and UK, or the CCPA in
California. Because Waypoint Journal doesn't collect or store personal data on our side, we hold no
personal data about you. We don't sell or share personal information, and never have. If you have any
questions about your rights, please contact us.

## Security

Your data stays in places Apple protects: your devices (with Apple's data protection) and your private
iCloud storage. Waypoint Journal doesn't send your data to any server of ours, because we don't have one.
